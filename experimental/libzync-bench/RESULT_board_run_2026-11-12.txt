WHAT THE RUN TAUGHT (each measured in this run)
  - Per-key claim cap. CLAIM_OPEN_MAX (default 4) limits open claims per key. Nine workers on one key proved ~100
    blocks/h until it was raised (12, then 24) for the run; it is back to 4.
  - The GPU lock. With HAZYNC_GPU_LOCK=none and six workers, one pod accepted 7 blocks in 5 min against 15 on each
    locked pod; peak GPU memory 25 GB. Early blocks are fixed per-block proving (trie ~0.26 M + aggregate ~3.1 M
    cycles + recursion), not GPU-bound; more workers or no lock do not help, more cards do.
  - Orphaned claims. Restarting a pod's workers mid-run left their claims on the shared key; such a claim looks alive
    for up to CLAIM_TTL (60 min) and the frontier stopped at each one (105, then 107..111, 118, ...). Proving the
    orphaned blocks directly (`hazync-worker run <n>`) cleared them; the frontier went from 110 to 792.
  - sm_120. The release CUDA host has no native RTX PRO 6000 (sm_120) kernels; the first prove on each fresh pod
    JIT-compiled from compute_100 PTX for ~10 min (the GPU smoke's 600 s timeout was hit once and retried).
